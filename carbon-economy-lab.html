Carbon economy

Money moves.
Carbon follows.

Every purchase activates a supply chain: materials, energy, transport, labour, buildings and services. Those activities create emissions somewhere.

The key idea: once those hidden emissions can be estimated, they can enter prices, targets, procurement decisions and carbon markets.

A simple spend-based model

$100 does not create the same footprint everywhere.

Spend-based models connect each purchase category to the average emissions created across its supply chain.

Think of it as a screening model: money → sector → supply chain → estimated kg CO₂e.
In this lab, everyone uses one common factor set. Real inventories can become more precise by using supplier, country and activity data.

Method + sources

GHG Protocol · Scope 3 Calculation Guidance ↗

Recognises supplier-specific, hybrid, average-data and spend-based methods. Spend-based estimates multiply economic value by EEIO emission factors.

Cornerstone EEIO 2026 ↗

Current multi-regional EEIO system with spend-based GHG factors for 405 sectors across 148 countries, regional averages and a global average.

US EPA · USEEIO ↗

Environmentally extended input-output modelling connects economic demand with supply-chain environmental impacts.

USEEIO spend-factor reference ↗

Classroom screening factors used in the spend calculator are derived from USEEIO 2024 purchaser-price factors. Examples include air transport 0.684, utilities 0.489, groceries 0.157, clothing 0.130 kg CO₂e per USD.

World Bank · State and Trends of Carbon Pricing 2026 ↗

87 implemented direct carbon-pricing policies, covering nearly 30% of global GHG emissions.

MSCI · Carbon Credits Come of Age in 2025 ↗

Primary market ≈$1.4bn in 2025; scenario range $5–20bn by 2030 and $60–270bn by 2050; 10,200+ projects across 18 major registries.

Verra Registry ↗

Public project, issuance and retirement records.

Gold Standard Impact Registry ↗

Public project, issuance and retirement records.

Puro Registry ↗

Durable carbon-removal projects and retirements.

ICCT · EU passenger-car lifecycle analysis 2025 ↗

Activity override factors: petrol 235 gCO₂e/km, hybrid 188, battery electric 63 on a lifecycle basis.

Calculator note. The spend model is a classroom screening tool, not an audited personal inventory. It uses one common factor set so students can compare logic without selecting a country. Real inventories should use the best available supplier, country, currency, year and activity data.
