Which areas are moving, which are too slow, and which are getting worse?

Make a quick team prediction. Then reveal the 2026 evidence.

Renewable power

Wind, solar and other renewable capacity.

Moving strongly.

Global renewable energy capacity increased 17% between 2024 and 2025. (UNFCCC, 2026c)

Electric vehicles

Share of new light duty vehicle sales.

Moving strongly.

Electric vehicles reached about one quarter of new light duty vehicle sales in 2025. (UNFCCC, 2026c)

Energy efficiency

Using less energy to deliver the same service.

Far too slow.

Improvement was about 1% a year, while roughly 4% is needed by 2030. (UNFCCC, 2026c)

Oil and gas methane

Methane released during fossil fuel production and supply.

Going the wrong way.

Oil and gas methane emissions increased in 2025. (UNFCCC, 2026c)

Early warning systems

Systems that warn people before major climate related hazards.

Improving.

Countries reporting multi hazard early warning systems rose from 108 to 119 between 2024 and 2025. (UNFCCC, 2026d)

Adaptation finance

Money helping people and places prepare for climate impacts.

Moving backwards in the latest data.

Finance tracked for adaptation fell from about USD 81.5 billion in 2022 to USD 64.1 billion in 2024. (UNFCCC, 2026e)

Implementation problem

A good idea can exist and still fail to spread

Imagine a city has a proven system that can reduce electricity waste and make the grid more flexible. Why might it still not scale?

The solution exists

A digital system can predict electricity demand and help match it with changing renewable supply.

The technology works in pilot projects. The city wants to expand it.

Select the three barriers you think could stop it.
Not enough grid infrastructure
Too expensive to deploy widely
Workers lack the skills to operate it
Rules and approvals are slow
Poor quality data
People do not trust the system
Choose three.

There is no single correct combination. The point is that technology rarely scales by itself.

COP31's implementation focus is essentially about this problem: policy, finance, infrastructure, skills and technology have to move together. (UNFCCC, 2026a; 2026b)
What is the IPCC doing now?

The next climate questions are already being studied

The IPCC is preparing its Seventh Assessment Report. You do not need the report structure. Just look at what scientists and governments have chosen to examine next.

Cities

Heat, flooding, transport, buildings, water and how people live.

Why now? More people live in cities, and many climate solutions and risks are concentrated there. A special IPCC report on cities is planned for 2027. (IPCC, 2026a)
Carbon removal

How to measure technologies that take CO₂ out of the atmosphere.

Why now? Removal is becoming part of Net Zero strategies, but quality and measurement matter. A methodology report is planned for 2027. (IPCC, 2026a)
Short lived climate pollutants

Methane and other pollutants that can strongly affect warming in the near term.

Why now? CO₂ is not the only climate problem. The IPCC plans a methodology report in 2027. (IPCC, 2026a)
The next global assessment

What has changed in climate science, impacts and solutions.

When? The AR7 Synthesis Report is planned for late 2029. (IPCC, 2026b)
Notice the shift: the next questions are not only “Is climate change real?” They are increasingly about what happens next, how impacts are managed, and how solutions are measured.

Use data and drones to support restoration.

Warn people before danger arrives.

Better climate data and communication can give people more time to act before floods, storms or extreme heat. The number of countries reporting multi hazard early warning systems is rising. (UNFCCC, 2026d)
🏭

Create a digital model of a real system.

Current climate implementation discussions include digital twins and machine learning to improve the efficiency of industrial assets. The value is not the digital model itself. It is the better decision that follows. (UNFCCC, 2026e)
Useful climate tool or digital decoration?

Judge the purpose, not the technology label

AI predicts when electricity demand will peak.

Grid operators use the forecast to reduce waste and manage renewable supply.

Useful if the forecast changes real operating decisions and the outcome can be measured.
A company adds “AI powered sustainability” to its website.

It gives no evidence of what changed.

Mostly marketing unless the company can show what problem the AI solves and what improved.
Satellite data detects a major methane leak.

The operator locates and fixes it.

Useful because information leads directly to an action that can reduce emissions.
An app sends climate facts to users.

No one knows whether behaviour changes.

Possibly useful, but awareness alone is not evidence of climate impact.
AI helps route emergency teams during a flood.

Road conditions and risk information update in real time.

Potentially very useful if the data are reliable and the system improves response.
AI generates thousands of personalised ads encouraging more consumption.

The company calls this “digital efficiency.”

Efficient for marketing does not automatically mean beneficial for climate action.
Simple test: what problem is being solved, what decision changes, and can the result be measured?
Technology can create a new problem

Digital does not automatically mean sustainable

Energy use

AI, cloud services and data centres require electricity and infrastructure.

Access

A useful digital solution is less useful if poorer communities cannot access the technology, data or skills needed to use it.

Bad data

A sophisticated model can still produce poor decisions if the information going into it is weak.

Misinformation

The same digital tools that spread useful information can also produce or amplify misleading climate claims.

COP31's 2026 climate action agenda explicitly includes AI and digital technologies, but also stresses responsible access, skills and information integrity. (UNFCCC, 2026e)
A new climate skill

Can you tell good information from climate noise?

AI makes producing convincing content easier. The management skill is not to believe or reject everything. It is to check.

Social post
“Scientists admit renewable energy does not work.”
Check: no source, no date, no study, no context. Treat as unverified until you find the original evidence.
UN Climate Change
“Renewable capacity increased 17% between 2024 and 2025.”
Check: named institution, current date, measurable indicator and traceable source. (UNFCCC, 2026c)
Company advertisement
“Our AI platform makes your business climate positive.”
Check: what exactly changed, compared with what, how was it measured, and was the result independently verified?
Source
Who is saying it?
Date
Is it current?
Evidence
What is the claim based on?
Original
Can you find the primary source?
The 2026 climate action agenda now treats information integrity as part of climate implementation, including concerns about AI enabled manipulation and misleading content. (UNFCCC, 2026e)

Look toward 2035

Which climate area could change fastest in the next decade?

Click one. Your task is not to predict the future perfectly. It is to identify what would have to happen for faster change.

Clean electricity

Already scaling quickly.

What must happen next? More grids, storage, faster approvals and better demand management.
Climate resilient cities

Planning is improving faster than physical resilience.

What must happen next? Finance, local delivery, heat and flood protection, better data and infrastructure.
Carbon removal

Technologies exist, but scale and credibility remain major questions.

What must happen next? Better measurement, lower costs, storage, infrastructure and credible demand.
Methane reduction

Solutions exist, yet progress is weak.

What must happen next? Detection, enforcement, repair, incentives and stronger implementation.
Early warning and adaptation

More systems are appearing, but exposure to hazards is still growing.

What must happen next? Reach more people, improve local response and finance actual resilience.
AI and digital climate tools

Growing quickly, but still difficult to measure globally.

What must happen next? Prove real outcomes, improve access and skills, and avoid creating new risks.
Why this matters to tourism and hospitality

Tourism sits inside all of these systems

You do not need another lesson about hotel thermostats to see the connection.

Destinations depend on cities and infrastructure.

Heat, flooding, water, transport and emergency systems affect whether places remain liveable and visitable.

Travel depends on energy and transport transitions.

The speed of change in electricity, mobility and fuels will reshape travel businesses.

Managers will buy and judge technology.

You may not build the AI model, but you may decide whether a proposed solution is useful, credible and worth paying for.

Climate information affects decisions.

Businesses need to tell the difference between evidence, marketing and misinformation.

Adaptation affects the product itself.

Some destinations will need to redesign seasons, experiences, infrastructure and visitor flows.

Implementation is a management problem.

Technology only works when finance, people, operations and policy line up.

Takeaways

Six ideas are enough

1. Climate action is not standing still.

Some areas are moving quickly.

2. Progress is uneven.

Some important areas remain too slow or are getting worse.

3. COP31 is about implementation.

The question is how to turn existing solutions into real outcomes.

4. The IPCC is looking ahead.

Cities, carbon removal, short lived pollutants and the next global assessment are part of the current work.

5. Technology is an accelerator, not the strategy.

A tool matters only if it changes a decision or outcome.

6. Scaling requires more than technology.

Infrastructure, finance, skills, trust and policy matter too.

Final question: by 2035, which climate solution do you think could change fastest, and what has to happen for it to scale?
