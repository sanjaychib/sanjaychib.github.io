Shared case · read this first

Larentia Events: corporate event sales

Larentia is a fictional premium hospitality group with hotels, event venues, restaurants and wellness operations. This exercise looks only at how it sells corporate events, not room bookings.

The situation

Companies send event enquiries through the website, email, sales partners and existing relationships. They range from leadership retreats to product launches and multi-day conferences.

Before a client can decide, the sales team must understand the brief, check venue fit and availability, clarify anything missing, prepare options and build a proposal.

Case data

Three business rules that cannot be broken

Availability comes from the live system.

Neither AI nor staff may estimate venue availability from old information.

Unclear briefs go back to the client.

If key information is missing or contradictory, the client must clarify before a proposal is completed.

Discounts have limits.

A sales manager may approve up to 10%. Anything larger needs the commercial director.

Step 2 · Compare options

Three ways AI could help, from light support to full autonomy

What you doOpen each option, then score all three.
WhyAll three are technically possible. They differ in value, effort and risk.
OutputYour group's preferred option, with reasons.
Option 1 · Assist

AI enquiry assistant

Reads incoming enquiries, extracts the key details, flags missing information and prepares a short brief for the sales team.

Low autonomy · people decide everything
Option 2 · Draft

AI proposal copilot

Uses the brief, venue information, pricing rules and past proposals to prepare options and a first proposal draft for sales to review.

Medium autonomy · people review and send
Option 3 · Act

AI sales agent

Responds directly to selected enquiries, prepares and sends offers, follows up and negotiates within set commercial limits.

High autonomy · AI acts with the client
Choose an option to see what it could improve and what it depends on.
Assistant: prepares information; a person acts on it.
Copilot: drafts the work; a person checks and sends it.
Agent: takes actions itself within rules it is given.
Pilot: a small, time-limited test before a full rollout.

Step 4 · Test it
Run three difficult situations through your workflow




What you doTrace each situation through the workflow you drew.


WhyA design that only works on easy cases will fail in practice.


OutputFix any route that is missing. No new workflow needed.







Situation 1

Conflicting guest numbers


The brief says 120 guests in one place and 220 in another.


Expected route: ask the client to clarify before the proposal continues.





Situation 2

Date already held


The live venue system shows another client already holds the preferred date.


Expected route: use the live system result, then offer another date or space, or refer to sales review.





Situation 3

A 15% discount request


The sales manager may approve only up to 10%.


Expected route: send the request to the commercial director for approval.






What this step shows: AI can support the process, but some decisions depend on live system data, client clarification or human authority. A good workflow makes those boundaries visible.
