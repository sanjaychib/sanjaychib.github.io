One story. Six connected questions.

Dimension 3 of 6 · Different businesses

Same industry. Different economics.

Compare a small independent property, a regional operator and a brand-affiliated hotel. All must sell rooms, but scale, resources, costs, distribution and access to capital differ.

Local / SME

Independent boutique hotel

Limited marketing scale. Faster local decisions. Direct owner exposure to cash pressure. Potentially distinctive guest experience.

Regional

Regional hotel operator

Some shared systems and purchasing benefits. Several markets or properties to balance. Regional brand awareness.

Corporate / brand

Brand-affiliated hotel

Brand standards, loyalty systems and distribution reach, but also contractual costs and less autonomy. Ownership and management can be separate.

These are stylised profiles, not claims about every business in each category. In practice, franchise, management and ownership arrangements vary.

Dimension 5 of 6 · Forecasts and budgets

What can we expect to sell, and what can we afford to do?

The first four dimensions showed why demand, costs and owner expectations change. Managers must now turn that uncertainty into a plan. Two tools help them do it.

Forecast: what are we likely to sell?

An evidence-based estimate of future bookings and sales for a specific period. Managers use reservations, past sales, events, competitor activity and changing demand to update it.

Why it matters: Expected bookings tell a hotel when to adjust prices, target new guests or prepare for a quieter month.

Budget: what is our financial plan?

A plan for expected income and spending. It helps managers decide what resources they can commit to staff, service delivery, marketing and longer-term investment.

Why it matters: A weaker forecast may require managers to reconsider spending, while protecting activities needed to generate and serve future sales.

How they fit together: Forecast expected sales → plan the resources and spending → act → compare actual results with the plan → revise. Here, you will work with a room-sales forecast and one sales spending budget, not prepare a full hotel financial statement.

Dimension 6 of 6 · Commercial choices

Now make the sale: without losing the business.

You have estimated next month's sales and planned how to use the sales spending budget. Now decide how to compete for bookings. The right action depends on demand, costs and what the owner needs.

Remember the difference: Your forecast estimates bookings and revenue. Your budget sets the spending available to support sales. Your strategy is the action you choose.

Watch the result: Does your sales action improve the hotel's financial position after relevant costs and its commercial spending? If actual bookings differ from the forecast, management will have to review the plan again.

Package: room-equivalent rate +7%, occupancy +2 points, incremental delivery cost CHF 14 per occupied room. These assumptions are invented for decision practice; they are not empirical estimates and they do not model capacity displacement, taxes, channel attribution or dynamic price elasticity.

Takeaway: adaptability is the commercial capabilityForecasts inform action; budgets limit and enable action; actual performance feeds back into the next forecast. There is no universally best sales strategy independent of demand, business economics, customers and owner priorities.
